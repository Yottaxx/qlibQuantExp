## Entry 1 — Adversarial verification corrected three of my own over-reads (and killed a phantom headroom)

Ran a 21-agent workflow (3 lenses × analyze→adversarial-verify) over the router/router_oracle/expert/temporal metrics across all 9 τ-scale arms. The verifiers were prompted to *refute*, and they earned their keep — three claims I had written into prior analysis turned out to be wrong, and one exciting "headroom" number turned out to be a measurement artifact. Recording the corrections here so future-me doesn't resurrect them.

### Correction 1 — `router_oracle_gap` is NOT recoverable alpha (kills the Track-5 framing)

**What I had said** (prior session message, ledger row 20): "oracle_expert_rank_ic_mean = 0.10–0.13 vs default 0.078 → huge headroom for Track 5 router calibration."

**Why it's wrong.** `oracle_expert_rank_ic_mean` is the per-day hindsight best-of-{time, factor}. It is structurally `router_oracle_gap = oracle − default` (verified to 1e-16). The decisive refutation: `default_beats_oracle_expert_day_ratio` = 0.337–0.488 — the realized soft mixture out-predicts the hindsight-selected single best expert on **34–49% of 611 days**. A genuine achievable ceiling can NEVER be beaten by the realized policy on any day. So the "ceiling" is a hindsight-selection artifact (max-of-2 inflation), not headroom a better gate could capture. The gap inflates wherever the per-day argmax has more variance to exploit — i.e., it's noise-amplitude, not skill.

**Lesson.** A max-over-k-experts "oracle" computed in-sample is upward-biased by exactly the selection noise. To get an honest ceiling you need split-half / OOS oracle (pick the expert on one half, score on the other). I almost sent us chasing a non-causal number.

### Correction 2 — `tau_drift = -2.687` at scale=5 is dispersion, not "downward drift across 2020→2022"

**What I had said** (Entry 4 of 2026-05-24, and the scale=5 analysis): "τ falls 2.69 across the eval window — instability; τ shortens memory exactly when 2022 stress should lengthen it."

**Why it's wrong.** `work_flow.py:1177` defines `tau_drift = st['last'] − st['first'] = tau[last single test day] − tau[first single test day]` — a **2-point endpoint difference**, not a smoothed trend and not "first-20% vs last-20%" (the SKILL.md description is aspirational/wrong). With daily `tau_std=2.041` at scale=5, a −2.69 endpoint gap is ≈1.3 daily SD — an unremarkable single draw from a 611-day high-variance series. I read a 2-sample noise draw as a regime trend. The narrative was fiction; the metric name `drift` is misleading.

**Lesson.** Always check the estimator definition before narrating a metric. "drift" sounded like a trend; it's a difference of two endpoints.

### Correction 3 — the scale=0.8 IR dip is NOT caused by L1 expert anti-correlation

**What I had said** (implied in the 9-point analysis): "scale=0.8 IR=1.475 dip co-occurs with L1 expert_cosine=−0.114 (destructive interference)."

**Why it's wrong.** scale=2.0 has the SAME-sign comparable L1 cosine (−0.096) but the BEST annualized return (0.189) and healthy IR (1.804). Same "effect", opposite outcome — the cosine→IR link is falsified internally. scale=0.8's RankIC is dead-average (0.0803); the IR trough is single-seed portfolio/turnover noise within the ~0.15 IR noise band. (The promotion implication — scale=0.8 is not a candidate — was right, but for the wrong reason; it's noise, not a real destructive-interference phenomenon.)

### Correction 4 (partial) — "extra τ spread is unanchored to regime" overstated

The volatility-correlation channel (`tau_vs_volatility_spearman`) does weaken as τ spread grows (−0.364→−0.250). But the tail channel (`tau_vs_tail_spearman`) RISES with spread (0.095→0.176, Pearson +0.73). So τ stays regime-anchored — it shifts WHICH regime axis it tracks, it doesn't decouple. The "regime homogenization at scale=5" worry was endpoint cherry-picking (regime/tail_rank_ic_spread vs util Pearson −0.03, minimum at scale=0.5 not high scale).

## Entry 2 — What survived and is genuinely new (high-confidence)

### The model is a time-expert with a vestigial-but-useful factor residual

Three confirmed findings compound into one picture:
- **Forcing either single expert hurts** (`router_time_advantage` −0.015..−0.040, `router_factor_advantage` −0.062..−0.098 at every scale). The factor expert solo is near-useless (RankIC ≈ 0, sometimes negative) yet load-bearing in the mix — its value is decorrelation/ensembling, not standalone skill. uniform 50/50 already beats time-only at 7/9 scales.
- **Effective time-dominance compounds**: router routes ~0.73 to time, AND the time expert's raw output norm is ~3.3× the factor expert's, so `contrib_norm_ratio` is 11–13× at layer 0. The "0.73 time fraction" headline badly understates how time-dominated the model actually is.
- **HC-2 (soft mixture) and HC-3 (axis-heterogeneous experts) are realized only in a degenerate, time-dominated form.** This is not a failure — the decorrelation residual genuinely helps — but the "two co-equal experts blending time and factor reasoning" story in the hard core is aspirational vs the measured 11:1 contribution reality. Worth a hard-core review: is HC-3 "experts are axis-heterogeneous" satisfied if one axis carries 90%+ of the contribution?

### τ operates on a channel orthogonal to attention — and was never "collapsed", only clamped

T1 (conf 0.95, source-verified): τ reshapes an additive, mean-normalized positional-embedding lag profile added to the token stream `h`; it never enters any attention logit. So the time-attention mass split (diag/local/long-range) being flat-invariant across all 9 scales is the EXPECTED signature, not evidence τ failed. Combined with the 9-point sweep showing strong adaptation once the `tau_mlp_out_scale=0.01` clamp is lifted, the durable "τ collapse" finding must be reframed: **τ was clamp-suppressed, not architecturally collapsed; it adapts strongly (p10–p90 [4.96,4.97]→[3.44,8.91]) once the gain is opened.**

### Open question for multi-seed (Phase 2)

Several mid-confidence findings need 3–5 seeds before trusting: (a) does τ adaptation push factor-solo skill positive as a real cross-channel coupling (R5, currently noise-level)? (b) is τ a load-REBALANCER between experts rather than a time-booster (R6, confound-flagged — time-solo drops while factor-solo rises)? (c) is the scale=0.5 L1_time_expert_norm spike (→36, the best-IR arm) a real richer-aggregation basin or a seed artifact (E5)? Phase 2's seeds {43-46} on scale 0.5 & 1.0 will resolve these as a side benefit.

## Entry 3 (2026-06-06) — The kill-check killed it: scale=0.5's IR edge was a 3-seed fluctuation

**Observed.** scale=0.5 looked like a clean promote at n=3 {42,43,44}: paired ΔIR +0.479, t=6.90, wins 3/3. We promoted it to conditional Tier-S, amended HC-6, then — per the owner's "add seeds first" call — ran the kill-check: 3 fresh seeds {45,46,47}, identical config. The pattern broke: s45 +0.030 (marginal win), **s46 −0.176, s47 −0.404 (losses)**. Paired ΔIR collapsed to **+0.148, t=0.92 (NS)**. The three fresh seeds alone are net **−0.183** (win 1/3).

**Expected.** I expected the 3/3 win to extend to 5/6 or 6/6 (interim 2-seed read had me leaning "signal嫌疑增大"). It did not.

**Three explanations (which is right?).**
1. **The n=3 result was a small-sample sampling fluctuation, full stop.** The t=6.90 was an artifact of a tiny within-cohort sd (0.120) on only 2 df — not a large effect. The discovery cohort {42,43,44} happened to all favor 0.5, and the fresh seeds reveal the truth: no significant IR edge. *This is the adjudicated reading (workflow `wf_31b7244d-2d8`), and it's almost certainly correct.*
2. **The "seed44 rescue" inflated everything.** baseline s44 IR=1.10 is a low outlier (other five baselines 1.38–1.97). The +0.617 "rescue" at s44 is largely regression-to-mean of an unlucky baseline draw, not a durable τ benefit — and s44 sat inside the discovery cohort, dragging the n=3 mean up. Remove that luck and the edge shrinks.
3. **There's a real but tiny effect the kill-check is underpowered to see.** At n=6, sd=0.395, detecting +0.148 at 80% power needs ~50 seeds. "NS" ≠ "no effect" — all of ΔIR/ΔAnnRet/ΔMaxDD stay positive at n=6 and nothing flips sign in aggregate. But "not clearly harmful" is not the bar for promotion; the burden is on the promotion.

**Next step.** Reversed the promotion (ledger row 62). τ-unclamp IR-edge logged as a NULL result at n=6. Kept the mechanistic finding (τ adaptation is real: tau_util 50×). The honest one-line: **"τ adaptation is real and active, but does not produce a seed-robust portfolio improvement on CSI300 single-split — mechanism ≠ benefit, the same trap as the original τ-collapse finding."**

**The methodological win.** This is exactly why the owner's "add seeds before walk-forward" instinct was right. The kill-check cost ~30 GPU-hr and prevented spending walk-forward + cost-stress + risk-overlay compute (Phase 3, ~hundreds of GPU-hr) on a non-effect. **A clean negative caught early is a success of the discipline, not a failure of the research.** Faraday's notebook records the nulls too.

**Residual thread (parked, not promoted).** scale=0.5 halves IR/MaxDD sample-variance (IR sd 0.323→0.263, MaxDD 0.0192→0.0098) and the worst-baseline-drawdown seeds improve most — a *suggestive* "adaptive τ damps regime-driven drawdowns" story. But it's NOT statistically established (F-tests NS) and single-split-fragile. If ever pursued, it needs a PRE-REGISTERED drawdown/tail criterion on fresh seeds + walk-forward, tested across τ scales {0.05, 0.1, 0.25} for dose-response — not a post-hoc rescue of one scale on one metric.
